# Market Intel — RTL / Private Lending, June 2026

Research snapshot to ground the plan. Items that couldn't be verified against a primary source
are marked (unverified). Compiled from web research June 10, 2026.

## 1. The market is growing — and the growth is in your lanes

- RTL originations exceeded **~$85B in 2025**, including $25B+ ground-up and $35B+ rehab
  (Urban Institute, unverified exact figures). Private lending overall growing ~30%/yr
  (Lightning Docs data, single source).
- Paradox to know cold: **flip counts hit a post-2020 low in 2025** (ATTOM: 297K flips, 25.5%
  gross ROI, lowest since 2008) — but lending volume grew anyway, on institutional capital,
  higher leverage adoption, ground-up, and DSCR. Sentiment has turned: John Burns' index jumped
  to 62 in Q4 2025 and **71% of flippers expect to buy more in 2026** (survey-record high).
- **Rates compressing:** avg bridge/RTL ~11.1% (Sept 2024) → 10.43% (Sept 2025), driven by
  rated-securitization capital. More capital chasing deals = pricing pressure on everyone, and
  speed/certainty matter even more as differentiators.
- **Hot segments: ground-up construction and DSCR.** DSCR is ~30% of record 2025 non-QM volume;
  investors bought a 5-year-high ~33% of single-family homes sold in 2025.

## 2. Where Genesis stands (use this in every recruiting call)

- **Record 2025: $4.8B originated, +33% YoY** (~$400M/mo firm-wide); four straight record
  quarters; **269 new sponsors added in 2025 (+66%)**. Your team's $75M/mo ≈ 19% of the firm.
- Genesis closed the **largest-ever rated RTL securitization ($500M, 2024)** — and the first
  rated deal focused on ground-up. Backed by Rithm's balance sheet. This is the certainty story.
- **Genesis rolled out Wholesale and Correspondent channels in 2025** — directly relevant to
  Engine 1: broker-sourced volume is the non-linear per-originator lever.
- CEO Clint Arrowsmith's public positioning: "lending to people, not just projects" — sponsor-
  based, repeat-driven. Your plan's repeat-borrower machine is on-strategy, not a side hustle.

## 3. The recruiting climate (this changes your pitch)

- **2026 is a growth/consolidation phase, not a distress phase.** The fire-sale talent windows
  (Civic 2023) have passed. Almost everyone credible is hiring: Anchor Loans did **$5.4B in
  2025, +117%** (Pretium); Kiavi did **$7.8B, +20%**, first to 100K loans; CV3 (Tessar) is near
  $2B and growing. You will be out-pitching healthy competitors, not catching falling knives.
- **The one soft spot: Lima One (MFA).** Restructured and cost-cut through 2024-25 (employee
  reviews cite layoff waves — anecdotal), now re-expanding (45 sales hires in 2025, wholesale
  launch 2026). Mid-tenure AEs who lived through the churn are the receptive profile — and your
  two existing contacts there are exactly that conversation.
- **Originators switch for production enablement, not bps:** the published reasons are slow
  turn-times, weak ops, narrow product menu, bad tech. LO mobility *slowed* in 2025 (stability
  premium). So the pitch that wins: concrete proof your platform grows their book — turn-time
  stats, draw speed, capital certainty, product width. Comp gets them to the table; enablement
  signs them.
- **Comp norms (directional):** consumer-retail LOs earn ~75-150 bps; wholesale AEs ~10-25 bps;
  RTL direct originators typically base $60-90K + 20-50 bps on funded (no authoritative survey
  exists — worth buying AAPL/NPLA comp data or asking a recruiter for hard numbers before
  setting your offer grid).
- **Benchmark for your roster screening:** your 7 average ~$129M/yr each — already elite vs the
  ~$10M/yr consumer-LO average. Screen candidates for $50M+/yr books with high repeat share;
  a $30M/yr producer needs your platform to double, which is fine *if* their sponsor
  relationships are real and portable.

## 4. Productivity benchmarks that justify Engine 1

- **Kiavi runs 84% repeat-transaction share** (2025). If your team is at 50-60%, the gap *is*
  the $9M of Engine-1 growth. Tactics that scaled shops use: enterprise sponsor tiers with
  rate/leverage step-ups, pre-approved exposure limits per sponsor, dedicated draw service for
  top sponsors, and **auto-outreach triggered at project payoff** — the moment a flipper needs
  the next loan.
- **Speed:** ~73% of private loans close in under 14 days (industry-cited, unverified). The
  <4-hour term-sheet SLA in the plan is table stakes done consistently, not heroics.
- **Cross-sell:** bridge → rehab → DSCR refi = 2-3 loans per project lifecycle. Every DSCR
  takeout your borrower closes elsewhere is sourced volume someone else funded.
- **Channel:** wholesale/correspondent is the industry-consensus 2026 play (Genesis launched
  2025; Lima One launching 2026; RCN built on it). If your team can plug into the firm's new
  channels, per-head volume becomes non-linear in personal effort.

## 5. Caveats

Renovo headline confusion (the Nov 2025 Chapter 7 was Renovo Home Partners, a remodeler — NOT
Renovo Financial the lender); Lima One layoffs are anecdotal against an official re-expansion
narrative; Urban Institute $85B and several scale figures unverified against primary pages;
RTL AE comp is inference from adjacent data, not a survey.
